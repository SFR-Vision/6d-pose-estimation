\section{Introduction}\footnote{Code and pretrained models: \url{https://github.com/SFR-Vision/6d-pose-estimation}}
\label{sec:intro}

Estimating the 6D pose of rigid objects from visual observations is fundamental to robotics and computer vision, with applications in robotic grasping, augmented reality, and autonomous manipulation. The task requires recovering 3D rotation and translation relative to the camera, which remains challenging due to occlusions, textureless surfaces, object symmetries, and the inherent ambiguity of monocular depth perception.

Recent learning-based methods have made significant progress on this problem. PoseCNN~\cite{xiang2018posecnn} pioneered end-to-end deep learning for pose estimation, directly regressing pose from CNN features. DenseFusion~\cite{wang2019densefusion} achieved strong results by combining RGB and depth through dense per-pixel feature fusion with iterative refinement. However, these methods either require complex multi-stage pipelines or expensive iterative inference.

We present a simpler two-stage framework for 6D pose estimation on LineMOD, comparing RGB-only and RGB-D approaches. Both use YOLOv8 for detection and segmentation, followed by a pose network. Our key contribution is \textit{geometric translation}: instead of regressing all translation components directly, we predict only Z-depth and derive X, Y analytically using the pinhole camera model. This explicitly embeds physical constraints into the learning pipeline, reducing the regression burden to rotation and depth estimation only.

For the RGB-D model, we further exploit depth sensor readings through a z-offset formulation---predicting small surface-to-center corrections ($\pm$10-30mm) rather than absolute depth (0.5-1.5m). This allows the network to leverage the sensor measurement as a strong geometric prior. We train both models using homoscedastic uncertainty weighting~\cite{kendall2018multi} to automatically balance rotation and translation losses.

Results show our RGB model achieves 41.2\% ADD-2cm accuracy, while the RGB-D model reaches 84.9\%---a 2$\times$ improvement. While our method does not match state-of-the-art iterative approaches on all objects, it excels on compact objects and offers simpler single-pass inference.
